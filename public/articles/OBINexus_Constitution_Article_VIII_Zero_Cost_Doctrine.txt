SECTION 1. DEFINITIONS

1.1  "Zero-Cost"
     A state of engineered unextractability in which the industrial-scale cost
     of essential infrastructure is reduced to capital net zero at the point of
     individual entry, achieved through zero-knowledge architecture and commons-
     based resource allocation. Zero-cost is not "free." Zero-cost is the
     deliberate elimination of extraction, not the concealment of it.

1.2  "Free" (Surveillance-Economy Definition)
     A marketing term designating a service in which the visible monetary price
     is set to zero while the true cost is distributed across all four Layers
     of extraction defined in Article VII, Sections 1.1 through 1.4. Under
     OBINexus commercial law, this term is classified as fraudulent when used
     without full disclosure of all four Layers.

1.3  "Zero-Knowledge Proof (ZKP)"
     A cryptographic method by which one party can prove to another that a given
     statement is true without conveying any information beyond the truth of that
     statement. In the Zero-Cost Doctrine, ZKP eliminates the need for identity
     exposure, behavioral profiling, or data harvesting as conditions of access.

1.4  "Capital Net Zero"
     The economic state in which the capital required from an individual node
     to access essential infrastructure — digital or physical — is driven to
     zero through systemic design, not through subsidy, charity, or debt
     instrument. The cost is absorbed by the commons, not by the user.

1.5  "Zero-Cost Abstraction"
     The architectural layer that renders the underlying infrastructure cost
     invisible to the user without transferring that cost to hidden extraction
     mechanisms. The abstraction is transparent in its methodology and
     verifiable in its execution.
